who: a developer whose notes name who is on the hook and which issue they belong to, in the
shape the repository already writes: `// TODO(@alice): #102 the retry is wrong`.

given: a Rust file with four notes — one `FIXME(@alice): (A) #102`, one
`TODO(@alice): (B) #PROJ-404 … @bob`, one whose parentheses hold a bare word, and one with
a space before the parentheses.

expect: three notes are listed and readable in place. A word in the parentheses that this
tool does not recognise is text, not a reason to drop the line: `TODO(carol): …` is a todo
with no assignee and no tag. The fourth note is not a todo at all — the parenthesis has to
touch the word.

why: `(@alice)` and `@alice` are two ways of writing one thing, and the tool has one list
of people and one list of tags — the parser puts the value in them rather than keeping a
second field that could disagree. `#102` is a tag because it is a `#` token: a number
somebody filed something under is still a tag, and a separate kind of `#` would need a
colour, a field and a key in every export to say nothing the tag list does not already say.

behaviour: the parentheses hold one `@person` or `#tag` and are read as one when they do;
anything else stays where it was written, in the lead, and the line is a todo as usual. They
are never a priority — a priority is written after the colon, where the body is. The text
stays in the line, so nothing is rewritten. RFC-021.
