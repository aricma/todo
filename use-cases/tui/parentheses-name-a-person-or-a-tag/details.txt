who: a developer whose notes name who is on the hook and which issue they belong to, in the
shape the repository already writes: `// TODO(@alice): #102 the retry is wrong`.

given: a Rust file with three notes — one `FIXME(@alice): (A) #102`, one
`TODO(@alice): (B) #PROJ-404 … @bob`, and one whose parentheses hold a bare word.

expect: two notes are listed and readable in place, the word in the parentheses is one of
the body's own `@person`/`#tag` tokens, `#102` is a tag like any other, and the third note
is not a todo at all — a bare word in the parentheses says nothing about which of the two
it is.

why: `(@alice)` and `@alice` are two ways of writing one thing, and the tool has one list
of people and one list of tags — the parser puts the value in them rather than keeping a
second field that could disagree. `#102` is a tag because it is a `#` token: a number
somebody filed something under is still a tag, and a separate kind of `#` would need a
colour, a field and a key in every export to say nothing the tag list does not already say.

behaviour: the parentheses hold exactly one `@person` or `#tag`; anything else, a space
before the `(`, or a `)` that never comes means the line is not a todo. They are never a
priority — a priority is written after the colon, where the body is. The text stays in the
line, so nothing is rewritten. RFC-021.
