who: a developer whose notes say who is on the hook and which issue they belong to, in the
shape the repository already writes: `// TODO(alice): #102 the retry is wrong`.

given: a Rust file with two notes — one `FIXME(alice): (A) #102`, one
`TODO(alice): (B) #PROJ-404 … @bob`.

expect: both notes are listed and readable in place; the person in the parentheses is in
the same list as the `@bob` in the body, one person is not counted twice, a ticket number
is a ticket and not a `#tag`, and the priority is still the `(A)` after the colon.

why: `(alice)` and `@alice` are two ways of writing one thing, and the tool has one list
of people — the parser puts them in it rather than keeping a second field that could
disagree. A `#102` in a tag list makes a collection called `102`, which is not a
collection anyone wants; a ticket number is what a person reads to go and look at the
issue.

behaviour: the parentheses are an assignee, never a priority — a priority is written
after the colon; the first ticket-shaped `#` token is the ticket and a second one is a
tag; the text of both stays in the line, so nothing is rewritten. The ticket leaves as
`aricma_ticket` in an OPS document, because the spec has no common key for one. RFC-021.
