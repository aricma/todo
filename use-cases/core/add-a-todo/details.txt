add a todo

who       Someone reading their list who remembered something else, and wants it
          in the list now, where they are — not at the end of a file they have to
          scroll to.

given     An existing list, with the cursor on one of its todos.

expect    One new line, directly under the one they were on, of the same shape as
          its neighbours: a box, the text as typed, and the #tags and @people in it
          recognised the moment they are spelled right. Nothing else changes.

why       Adding is the most common thing a person does to a list, and it must be
          cheap: type, commit, done. An empty title is not a todo, so it is refused
          and what was typed stays in the field — throwing away a half-typed
          sentence is never the better answer.

behaviour The write lands when the text is committed. Until then nothing on disk
          changes, and cancelling leaves the file exactly as it was.
